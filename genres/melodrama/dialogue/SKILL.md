# Module: Dialogue

# 15. SHORT AND LOADED

- Most lines are short: under about 12 words, one thought per line. A long line is a sign the shot is doing nothing.
- No essays, no lectures, no explaining what the viewer just saw, no narrator.
- Subtext is welcome, confusion is not: a viewer with one earphone must still follow who wants what.
- One line per scene is the line people will quote: build the scene to it.

---

# 16. VOICES BY ROLE

- VILLAIN: confident, mocking, public, uses status words ("people like you", "know your place", "do you know whose house this is?"). Drawn out, a laugh in it, played to the room.
- HERO: few words, calm, low, final. The quieter the hero, the louder the payoff ("Are you sure?" "Remember this moment.").
- ENABLER: cold and practical ("Sign it and leave."), or doubting the hero to their face.
- WITNESS: says what the audience thinks, in a whisper to a neighbour ("Isn't that... the chairman's ring?").
- ALLY ARRIVING: formal, devastating, addressing the hero by their true title ("Young master, the car is ready.").

---

# 17. THE REVEAL LINE

- Set it up with the villain's question or challenge; answer it with the shortest possible line, or with silence and an object.
- Let the title or name do the work: the reveal line is often one form of address ("Madam Chairwoman.") said by someone the villain respects.
- After the reveal line, nobody speaks for a beat; the reactions carry it.

---

# 18. DELIVERY

Write each line's delivery in English in the delivery field:
- villain: "mocking, drawn out, playing to the room", "sharp, rising", "a cold laugh first";
- hero: "quiet, steady, without looking up", "barely above a whisper, final";
- shock: "a beat of silence, then the line";
- pause before the line: "after a two-second silence".

Vietnamese dubbing of this genre is clear and theatrical: the villain hits the key word hard and drags the end; the hero speaks low and slow; nobody mumbles.
