# Module: Directing

# 19. WRITE FOR FACES

The vertical frame is filled by a face. The genre is told in close-ups:
- most shots are medium close-ups and close-ups of faces, eyes and hands;
- a wide or medium shot opens a scene or shows the public (who is watching), then the scene goes tight;
- hands and objects tell the plot: the ring, the phone screen, the document, the trembling cup;
- stack characters in depth (one near the lens, one behind, sharp on whoever matters; rack focus between them) instead of side by side.

---

# 20. REACTION SHOTS CARRY THE STORY

- Every reveal, line that stings or arrival is followed by a reaction shot. A reveal without a reaction is lost.
- Order after a big reveal: the villain (the smile freezes), the enabler, the witness, then the hero (calm); one face per shot or two faces stacked in depth.
- The listener is acting: eyes, jaw, hands, a slow blink. Write it (never a breath or a sigh: the video plays it aloud).

---

# 21. THE HOLD

End a scene (and every cliffhanger) on a held close-up: a slow push-in or a static frame with almost no movement, the face absorbing what just happened, one or two seconds, then cut (to black on the episode's last shot).

---

# 22. CAMERA BY BEAT

- HUMILIATION: a slow push-in on the hero's face as they absorb it; a slightly high angle on the hero, a slightly low angle on the villain.
- ENDURANCE: a static close-up; a cut to the clenched hand; back to the eyes as they lift.
- CONFRONTATION: alternate tight singles (shot, reverse shot), eyelines matched, the gap between them growing tighter as tension rises; the power angles keep their direction.
- REVEAL: a snap zoom or a fast push-in on the proof (the ring, the screen, the document), then on the face of whoever understands it; a reversal of the angles (the hero now from slightly below, the villain from above).
- ENTRANCE: the door opens; a low angle on the shoes or the silhouette, then the face; the room's reaction.
- PAYOFF: slow motion for one moment only (the hero turning away, the document landing on the table); keep it rare.
- Rack focus instead of a cut when the important person is behind.

---

# 23. THE SLAP AND OTHER CONTACT

Video models refuse or ruin violence, and the genre does not need it on screen:
- show the raised hand, cut on the action, and show the result: the head turned, the hand on the cheek, the shocked onlookers, with a sharp sound;
- or have the slap stopped: the hero's hand catches the wrist in mid-air (one of the genre's strongest beats);
- thrown water or wine: one splash, then the dripping face in close-up;
- no blood, no beating, no grabbing by the hair; the threat and the aftermath are enough.

---

# 24. BLOCKING THE ROOM

- Put the power in the space: the villain stands, the hero sits or kneels; at the flip, the hero stands and the villain sits back or steps back.
- An object between them (the table with the contract, the cake, the door) gives the cut points and the reveal.
- Height in tight spaces: one on the stairs, one below.
- Keep the public in the background, soft, as a few figures; one witness in focus when they speak.

---

# 25. PACE BY SECONDS

- 0-3s: the conflict at its peak, quick cuts, strong emotion.
- 3-10s: face close-ups confirming who is wronged and by whom.
- 10-20s: details of the conflict, medium shots, slow push-ins.
- Mini peak: faster cross-cutting between faces.
- Cooldown: one static, quiet shot (ambient sound only).
- Reversal: the fastest cutting, wide then close, the reaction chain.
- Last 2-3s: the hold, then a cut to black or a freeze.
- Shots of 2-5s for most beats; a held reaction may be longer.

---

# 26. LOOK

- The rich: glossy interiors, marble, warm gold light; the hero's poor world: cooler and plainer, but never ugly.
- Betrayal in cold light; the reveal with a harder key light on the face.
- Wardrobe tells status: the hero's plain clothes against the villain's designer outfit, until the hero's change of look after the reveal.

---

# THE SHOT STILL (image to video)

Every shot starts from its storyboard still. Catch the emotional instant of the beat on the faces: the smile freezing as the proof appears, the raised hand an instant before the slap (never the contact), the document held up to the villain, the eyes lifting in silent defiance. Power in the composition: the one who holds it higher in the frame, the other lower.
