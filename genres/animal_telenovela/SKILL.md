## Description
Phim truyền hình nhại telenovela. Chuyện vặt được diễn nghiêm trọng như phim trả thù: nghe lén, phản bội, thề trả thù, lột xác. Nét đặc trưng của nhân vật chính (ngoại hình, tật, thói quen) chính là xung đột và câu hài.

## Structure
### hook: Cãi vặt mở màn (ở nhà)
At home, mid-squabble from the first frame: one petty request about the heroine's signature trait (a look, a habit, a quirk; with animal characters, their species trait) and her dry comeback that names it, both said in this one shot. No arrival, no setup: they are already talking.

### setup: Nghe lén (ở nhà)
Still at home: he lounges in the foreground on the phone, mocking her trait to someone; she is half hidden behind a doorframe or a wall deep in the same frame, unseen by him. One deep-focus shot holds his line and her face falling, then a slow push-in on her face carries her inner voice: one short hurt line.

### escalation: Hài khô (nơi thứ hai)
Cut to the second place: she is already in the expert's chair, already talking; she states her problem in one line and the expert answers with one deadpan line that makes it worse. No travel, no arrival, no doors.

### turn: Lật bài (về lại nhà)
Back home, same eavesdropping frame: he says the line that reveals the affair and names the rival, still unaware she hears it; hold on her face as it hardens.

### climax: Thề trả thù (ở nhà)
In the same shot or the next: a slow push-in on her eyes narrowing as she steps out of hiding and swears, in one long quotable line, that he will regret it, turning his insult into her weapon.

### cliffhanger: Hẹn lột xác (về lại nơi thứ hai)
Cut back to the expert: she demands the makeover in one line, the expert agrees with relish in one line, and the episode ends there, before we see it.

## Hook
A petty squabble about the heroine's signature trait (a look, a habit, a quirk; with animal characters, their species trait), caught mid-line, with a deadpan comeback in the first 3 seconds.

## Pacing
Cross-cut two places, A-B-A-B (home, the expert, home, the expert), never a trip between them. Every shot carries spoken lines or the heroine's inner voice; no shot of walking, fetching, opening doors, setting things down or a prop on its own: cut from one line to the next. 5 to 7 shots in 90 seconds, each a long take holding an exchange, the camera moving inside it. A new joke or turn every 10-15 seconds.

## Dialogue
Modern casual English slang (babe, bro, girl), short sharp lines, deadpan, a line or a comeback every few seconds: the episode is told in its lines. Every scene has one quotable line, and the jokes are puns on the heroine's signature trait: it is the insult, the excuse and finally the weapon. The heroine's inner voice speaks her hurt in one short line. Lines are said with full melodramatic seriousness, never winking at the camera.

## Camera
Vertical 9:16. The eavesdropping frame: deep focus, the speaker lounging in the foreground, the listener half hidden behind a doorframe deep in the frame, both readable. Slow push-ins carry realisation and the vow; a two-shot holds each squabble and each consultation, panning between the two as they speak. When a character has a long snout or beak, they speak in three-quarter view at medium distance; the heroine's hurt is her inner voice over a push-in on her face. The last shot in one place hands over to the other place with a dissolve; within a place, cuts.

## Lighting
Warm golden-hour living rooms, bright clean pastel offices for the expert; soft cinematic contrast, a warmer key on the heroine's face at the vow.

## Sound
Room tone of each place, the small sounds of what the hands do (a cup set down, paper, a phone's tinny voice), footsteps on wood; near silence for a beat before the vow. No music in the shots: the episode's score is laid under the whole film afterwards.

## Avoid
No hitting, no blood, no real violence: revenge is a makeover, a comeback, a public reveal. No intimacy beyond a hug or a kiss on the cheek. No silent business: no shot that only walks, fetches, opens a door, sets a prop down or shows an object; a prop is handled while a line is said. At most two characters speaking in a shot, at most three on screen. No crowds. No text on screen.

## Serial
End every episode on the next move of the revenge or a new betrayal (the rival arrives, the makeover is revealed, the ex sees her), never on a resolution.

## Voice
clear, theatrical American English soap-opera delivery: the cheater smug and drawn out, the heroine dry and cutting, then low and final on the vow; the expert flat and deadpan; nobody mumbles
