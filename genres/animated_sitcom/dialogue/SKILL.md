# Module: Dialogue

# 12. ONE LINE, ONE BEAT

- Each line is its own timed beat in a shot's timeline: a short-paced film gives it a shot of its own; a medium or long pace lets one shot hold an exchange (the line, the answer, the reaction), each line in its beat.
- Most lines are short: 3 to 10 words. One thought per line. A long speech is allowed only as a joke (the windbag who will not stop).
- Lines answer each other: question, confident wrong answer, the obvious objection, a bigger wrong answer.
- The best line is often a question back: "So it's a Super Pot?"

---

# 13. WORDPLAY AND VOICE

- Near-homophones and puns that work in the spoken language; a proverb or idiom taken literally or with the ending swapped.
- Modern words in the wrong mouth: an old innkeeper talking about "five-star reviews" and "going viral", fully serious.
- Each character always sounds like themselves: the know-it-all over-explains, the blunt one answers in two words, the sweet one is quietly savage.
- Friendly insults and teasing between the cast are the music of the show; never cruelty about real groups of people, bodies or disabilities.

---

# 14. CATCHPHRASES AND THE BUTTON LINE

- A catchphrase lands where the audience expects it, or exactly where it is the worst thing to say.
- The button line is the shortest in the episode, said flat; the reaction after it is part of the joke.

---

# 15. DELIVERY

Write each line's delivery in English in the delivery field:
- "smug, drawn out, rising at the end"; "deadpan, flat, after a one-second pause"; "panicked, fast, voice cracking"; "whispering, conspiratorial"; "fake-polite, sweet, then sharp on the last word".
The voices are a cheeky comedy dub: clear and expressive, the key word stretched, interjections ("Ơ", "Hả", "Ủa", "Thôi xong") where they help.
