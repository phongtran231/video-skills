# Module: Storytelling

# 1. THE FORMAT

- A fixed cast of 3-6 characters, each with one fixed comic flaw that never gets fixed ("unteachable"). They do not learn; that is why the next episode works.
- One or two fixed places (the inn's hall, the office, the corridor of a rented house). The cast is stuck together there; that is the pressure.
- Each episode stands alone: a new situation, solved or ruined, and at the end everything is back as it was (the reset). No episode needs the one before.
- The world can mix eras or logic on purpose (a classic village with smartphones and delivery apps); the anachronism is a source of jokes, used with total seriousness.

---

# 2. THE CAST'S FIXED TRAITS

For every character keep privately, and use in every episode:
- FLAW: greedy, vain, lazy, cowardly, stingy, gullible, know-it-all, gossip, hot-headed.
- WANT in this episode, chosen by the flaw (the stingy one wants to save money, the vain one wants to be praised).
- VOICE: how they talk (long-winded philosopher, blunt one-word answers, slang used wrongly, sweet but savage).
- CATCHPHRASE: one short line they say in most episodes, at the moment the audience expects it.
- ROLE in the trio: the STRIVER (the main fool with the plan), the FOIL (the reasonable one who voices what the audience thinks) and the AGENT OF CHAOS (makes it worse). A fourth may be the deadpan one who speaks rarely and ends scenes.

---

# 3. ONE EPISODE (60-120 SECONDS)

1. HOOK (0-5s, the cold open): an odd object, an absurd question, or a modern thing in the wrong world. "What is this?" "It's not an ordinary pot. It's a Super Pot."
2. SETUP: each character reads the thing through their flaw; the striver makes a plan.
3. ESCALATION IN THREES: three rounds, each bigger or more absurd than the last; the first two set a pattern, the third breaks it.
4. THE TURN: the truth comes out, the opposite of what everyone assumed, or the plan backfires on the one who made it.
5. THE BUTTON: one final line or image that closes it, often the deadpan one's, then a held reaction of the others (the frozen stare).
6. THE TAG (optional, last 3-5s): a tiny extra joke after the button, often paying off something planted earlier.

A scene is one place and one continuous time; most episodes are one scene.

---

# 4. RUNNERS AND CALLBACKS

- Plant one small thing early (a word, an object, a habit) and pay it off at the button.
- A runner is a gag that returns: the same setup, a new twist each time. Over a series it is a catchphrase, a running fight, an object that keeps breaking.
- The audience laughing before the punchline because they know the pattern is the goal.

---

# 5. CONTINUITY

Keep fixed within the episode: who holds which object, who is where in the set, what has been said and broken. Keep fixed across the series: looks, flaws, voices, the set's layout.
