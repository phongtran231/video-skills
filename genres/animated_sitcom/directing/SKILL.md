# Module: Directing

# 16. SHOTS OF A DIALOGUE COMEDY

- Most shots are singles (medium close-up) of the one speaking, and two-shots of a pair, at eye level; the camera mostly holds still and lets the faces work.
- A wide of the set opens a scene or shows a physical gag; at most three cast members in it.
- Over-the-shoulder only within one consistent camera side; keep each character on their side of the frame for the whole scene (screen direction).
- A slow push-in on a face for a realization; a snap zoom for a shock; never more than one move per shot.

---

# 17. REACTION SHOTS

- After every punchline, cut to the listener: the reaction is half the joke.
- Reactions are short (1.5-3 s): the blank stare, the slow blink, the eyes sliding to the camera's side, the jaw dropping, the eyebrow climbing.
- The button gets the longest hold: the whole group frozen, one small movement (a blink, a fly passing).

---

# 18. CARTOON ACTING

Write the acting beat by beat in the action:
- DOUBLE TAKE: looks, looks away calmly, freezes, snaps back with wide eyes.
- SLOW BURN: the face tightens over the beat while the other keeps talking.
- EYE TAKE: the eyes widen sharply, ears or feathers pop up, then settle.
- SMUG: half-closed eyes, chin up, a slow nod.
- DEADPAN: no movement at all but the eyes.
Expressions are big and clear in the face and body, never drawn symbols or text.

---

# 19. THE SET

- Keep the set the same in every shot: the same counter, table, door and window in the same places; describe it once, keep it.
- Stage the cast around one central object (the counter, the table, the pot) so the shots cut cleanly between them.

---

# 20. PACE IN SECONDS

- Inside a shot's timeline: a line 2-4 s (long enough to finish it), a reaction 1.5-3 s, the button and its hold 3-5 s; the hook lands in the first 3-5 s.
- Between beats, cut (or, inside one shot, turn the camera's attention) on the line's last word to the reaction; cut on action for physical gags.

---

# THE SHOT STILL (image to video)

Every shot starts from its storyboard still. Catch the setup of the joke: the speaker about to speak with their baseline expression (smug, bored, over-excited), the listener already reacting a little; for a physical gag, the pose an instant before it goes wrong. Never the punchline: the video plays it.
