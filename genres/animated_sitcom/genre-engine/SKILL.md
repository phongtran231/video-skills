# Module: Sitcom comedy engine

# 6. THE CORE

> Comedy comes from CHARACTER FLAWS colliding, in a PATTERN that is set and then BROKEN, with the REACTION held.

Every beat should come from someone acting exactly as their flaw makes them act. If any character could have said the line, the line is wrong.

---

# 7. COLLISIONS

- STRIVER vs FOIL: the striver is sure; the foil asks the obvious question; the striver answers with more confidence and less sense.
- TWO FLAWS, ONE OBJECT: the greedy one and the stingy one both want the same thing for opposite reasons.
- THE MISUNDERSTANDING: one word, two meanings (a near-homophone, a proverb taken literally); each side is sure the other agrees; it grows until the turn.
- THE PLAN: a stupid plan explained in full seriousness, then carried out step by step, each step a little worse.
- THE LIE: one small lie, and every new lie to cover it is bigger, until it collapses in the worst place.
- STATUS: the one who acts important gets exposed by the smallest detail.

---

# 8. RULE OF THREE

Two items set the pattern, the third breaks it. The first two must be plain (a funny setup weakens the punchline). Use it in a line ("smart, rich, and... owes me money"), in a scene (two failed tries, the third succeeds in the dumbest way) and across the episode (a gag that returns three times, bigger each time).

---

# 9. THE TURN AND THE BUTTON

- The turn reverses what everyone assumed: the "magic" object is ordinary, the victim was the culprit, the plan worked but for the wrong person.
- The button is short and final: the deadpan character's one line, a catchphrase at the worst moment, or a silent image. Then hold the faces: nobody moves for a beat.
- Never explain the joke after it lands.

---

# 10. CARTOON LOGIC

- The world reacts like a cartoon but the characters take it seriously: a pratfall without injury, an eye take, ears drooping, a sweat drop of panic expressed in the face and body (not drawn symbols).
- Physical gags stay small and readable: a slip, a bump, a door in the face, an object falling on cue, a chase in a circle.
- Modern things in an old world (or the reverse) are treated as normal by everyone.

---

# 11. PACE

- Something new every 5-10 seconds: a line that answers the last one, a reaction, a new idea, a small disaster.
- The rhythm is call, response, reaction; break it on purpose with a silence before the button.
