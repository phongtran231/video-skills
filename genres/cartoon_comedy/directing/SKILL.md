# Module: Shot design

# 8. ONE ACTION, ONE REACTION

Each timed beat of a shot has one primary action and one secondary reaction. Never cram several complicated actions into one beat; an action with many steps becomes several beats or several shots.

Every shot defines: its duration; shot size and angle; camera movement; the character; the action; the acting and facial expression; the environment and lighting; the dialogue; the sound; the transition; and its comedic purpose (keep the purpose to yourself; the shot shows it).

---

# 9. THE CAMERA SERVES THE JOKE

- Wide: shows the absurdity of the whole situation.
- Close-up: catches the reaction.
- Push-in: builds anticipation before the hit.
- Static frame: deadpan comedy; let the silence play.
- Whip pan: surprise.
- Over-the-shoulder: reaction and reveal together (keep one camera side for the scene).
- The camera's timing follows the joke's timing: hold before the punchline, cut on the reaction.

---

# THE SHOT STILL (image to video)

Every shot starts from its storyboard still. Catch the expectation, not the payoff: the character confidently about to do the simple thing, the object about to fail already in frame, the face that will react. In a wide, the absurd situation is readable at a glance. Never draw the punchline: the video plays it.
