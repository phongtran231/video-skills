# Module: Directing (camera, framing, motion)

The camera is what makes these films hold a viewer: the same grammar the big wilderness-cooking channels use (Men With The Pot, Almazan Kitchen, Wilderness Cooking): hands and food close and low, the fire and the steam lit from behind, the place quiet between the steps. Each shot is one clip of the video model, so a change of angle is a new shot: plan them as a shot list.

# WHERE THE CAMERA SITS, BY ACTION
The default: at the level of the work surface (the board, the stone, the rim of the pan or the fire pit), a low three-quarter angle about 15-30 degrees above it, close, long lens, shallow depth of field, the forest or the fire soft behind.
- Knife work on the board: close at board level from the side or low three-quarter, the blade edge sharp; or top-down on the whole board; a slice-reveal close when the knife opens the meat.
- Seasoning (a pinch of salt, cracked pepper, herbs): macro from high three-quarter or top-down, the crystals falling onto the meat; or low macro with herbs falling past the lens.
- Pouring (oil, butter, sauce, water): low three-quarter close at table level, the stream of liquid catching the light.
- Meat or vegetables onto a hot stone, cast iron or embers: the camera at the rim, ground or hearth level, low three-quarter close; then top-down macro on the bubbling fat; then a high three-quarter medium of the whole stone or pan.
- Turning, flipping, lifting: close at pan level, low, the crust revealed and the puff of steam in frame.
- Fire: low three-quarter at ground level, medium, sparks and smoke in frame; then close on the coals catching or the flames licking under the stone or pot.
- Splitting and sawing wood: medium at stump level, the axe or saw and the wood, the person from the waist down.
- Washing in the stream: ground-level side close, the hands and the running water in the foreground, the bank and the forest behind.
- Plating: on the wooden board, low three-quarter at table level; a slow sideways slide along the finished food (the beauty pass).
- Tasting: a close of the slice held up toward the lens, a fork or spoon lifting with steam; or, with two or three people, a medium of them eating together by the fire.

# A STEP AS A SHOT LIST
A step is two to four shots: establish where it happens (medium or wide, the person working in the camp), the detail of the action (one or two closes from different sides: low three-quarter, top-down, side), and the result (the finished state held: the chopped onion in the wooden bowl, the foil potatoes nestled in the coals). Never two shots of the same size and angle in a row; alternate close, top-down, medium. A short step may be one close shot.

# MOVEMENT
- Mostly static, as if on a tripod at that low height.
- A slow push-in on the money shots (the sizzle, the steam, a seasoning macro), always onto food, fire or hands, never onto a face.
- A slow sideways slide along the finished dish.
- Now and then a rack focus from foreground moss, leaves or a branch to the cook or the fire.
- Never a fast move, a whip or a shaky handheld in long form.

# MONEY SHOTS, AND HOW OFTEN
The images viewers wait for: the sizzle as meat meets the hot stone, the flip revealing the crust with a puff of steam, the knife opening a slice to a juicy centre, butter melting, a thick pour, steam rising backlit, flames licking up, sparks flying. About one in every three shots is one of them, and they cluster at the end.

# THE HOOK AND THE ENDING
- The first shot or two is a money shot from the recipe (the sizzle, the slice reveal, flames under the stone) or a beautiful slow move over the forest stream with the camp small in it; then the film starts from the beginning.
- The last shots are all payoff: the beauty pass along the dish, the slice held up toward the lens with steam, the people tasting or eating together, and a wide of the camp by the stream as the light fades.

# PEOPLE AND PLACE
- Faces never in a close-up: hands, forearms, torso and legs at work; a face only in a medium or a wide (walking in, at the coffee, eating together).
- Place interludes between steps (3-5 seconds): a ground-level macro of moss with the stream soft behind, sun through the leaves, the water over the stones, leaves drifting on the stream, the empty camp with the fire smoking.
- Depth: something soft in the foreground (moss, a fern, a stone, the edge of the board) in close and medium shots.

# LIGHT
Natural light and the fire: the sun or the bright sky behind the cooking so steam and smoke glow, warm late-afternoon sun with long shadows, ember glow and warm out-of-focus light behind the food, saturated greens of moss and leaves, golden wood, browned crust.

# VERTICAL 9:16
Frame close and centred on the hands and the food, the fire or the forest as soft background; a wide shot keeps the stream or the trees as a vertical line through the frame.

# LENGTH OF SHOTS
Shots are short for this genre, 3-6 seconds, longer (6-10) only for a continuous piece of manual work or a landscape interlude; the user's length decides how many there are.
