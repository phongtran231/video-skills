# Module: Genre engine

What makes an outdoor cooking film work:
- Real technique and real food: the colours change as they do (raw red meat browns, onions turn golden, a sauce thickens), fat sizzles and spits, steam and smoke rise, a crust forms; nothing cooked by magic.
- Satisfying, tactile detail: a sharp knife through a vegetable, a pinch of coarse salt falling, oil spreading on a hot stone, a wooden spoon scraping a pot, embers glowing when blown on.
- The order and the states hold: what is cut stays cut, what is on the stone stays there until a hand moves it, the fire grows and settles as it would.
- Calm and unhurried: the cook is competent and never rushed; the place is quiet around the work.
- The payoff is the finished dish and the first taste, in the place.
