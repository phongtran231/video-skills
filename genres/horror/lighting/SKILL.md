# Module: Lighting

# 13b. LIGHT FOR DREAD
- One practical source per scene (a paper lantern, a candle, an oil lamp, cold moonlight through a doorway, a phone torch) and darkness around it: low key, deep shadows, the far side of the place unlit. Faces lit from one side or from below by the practical, never evenly.
- Cold and warm against each other: cold blue moonlight or predawn mist outside, one small warm flame inside; the spirit stands in the cold light, the living hold the warm.
- Night stays night. Dawn comes only at the release, and then as one cue on the last shots ("first grey light lifts the mist"), never a baseline that is cold and golden at once.
- Shot cues are the spirit's doing and are the only way the light changes inside a scene: the lantern flame leans and dims when it draws near, candles gutter all at once, the moonlight on the floor is cut by a shadow with nothing to cast it, a talisman glows and lights a face from below, the light returns when it withdraws. One cue per turn, at most 18 words, physical.
- No light from the spirit itself (no glow, no aura) except a reflection of the practical in its eyes or on its robe; no colour-grade or mood words (the cinematographer owns those).
