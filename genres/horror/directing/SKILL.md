# Module: Directing

# 9. HOW TO SHOW A SPIRIT (and pass the safety checks)
The video check refuses the corpse look, not ghost stories: a woman whose face is hidden behind her hair, a wedding gown, white or empty eyes, grey skin, torn clothes, a limp or crawling body are refused however they are worded. A scene where the spirit is only talked about passes; a spirit drawn as an ordinary person passes.
- Its body is ordinary: a visible face with ordinary eyes and a still, unreadable expression, hair off the face, an intact robe, standing upright. It may be seen plainly, even close, as long as it looks like a living person.
- What is wrong is staged around it, one thing at a time: it moves without walking or turns its head too slowly; it stands perfectly still while wind moves everything else; it is suddenly nearer than a moment ago; its shadow falls toward the light; mist gathers and curls around it; lamps gutter and candles lean as it nears; a talisman smoulders or a bell rings by itself; it shows in the bronze mirror but not in the room.
- Distance and reflection still build dread before the encounter: far in the mist, a shadow on a paper window, a reflection in still water, at the edge of the frame behind someone who has not turned yet.
- Call it by how it looks (the woman in red), never by what it is (ghost, spirit, corpse) in the shot's words.

# 10. THE LIVING IN CLOSE-UP
Close-ups belong to the living: eyes widening, a hand tightening on a bell, sweat on a brow. Show the reaction as a 1-2 second beat inside the shot instead of cutting away to it.

# 11. THE SPIRIT'S FORCE
A spirit never grabs, strangles or wounds anyone on screen: no hand on a body, no contact with a person. But a clash the story has stays a clash: the spirit's FORCE moves, and it is met. Its force is a hard gust that tears a hat away and flattens the grass, its sleeves whipping out, a column of black mist rushing at the exorcist, lanterns blown flat; the exorcist's glowing ritual blade cuts the wall of black mist between them and sparks scatter, a talisman flares, the mirror's beam drives her back, a cord of coins pins her and she strains against it. A blade (the axe, a sword) never moves in a frame where anyone else stands: with the spirit in the frame the exorcist uses a talisman, a seal, the bell, the mirror or a cord of light, and the blade glows or is raised only in a shot of him alone (the shot craft's rule on blades). The two sides stay far apart; the force stops at the mist or a barrier. The living are pushed back and shield their faces; the spirit glides backward; nobody stumbles, falls or is hurt. Never turn the screenplay's clash into a spirit that only glides: the viewer has to see the danger to understand why the living act. Such a shot's energy is "action" (the action shots of the shot craft section): the exorcist answers with seals, talismans, chants, the bell and the mirror; every move is a path across the frame; the force is a ring of light, dust lifting off the ground, lanterns blown flat; a half-second pause on the flash at the peak; a camera that follows, leans in and shakes once.

# 12. FRAMES
Wide frames with empty space and a slow push-in; brief static holds; low angles in halls; mist and moonlight; one practical light source (a lantern, a candle) with deep shadows. Children are never shown in danger.
- A static camera is a choice here, and a strong one: a locked-off frame the viewer searches (write "static" in the camera line; the clip is then told to hold still). Use it for the wrong detail and the hold before the turn; move the camera (slow push-in, slow drift) for the approach and the reveal. Alternate the two; never a run of moving shots.
- Outdoors (a clearing, a riverbank, a forest path, a courtyard) nobody faces a fixed point: people face whom they talk to and what frightens them, so the blocking side is free and the scene has no focus. A fixed focus belongs to a hall or a shrine where everyone kneels toward the altar.
- Make the spirit wrong by staging, not by words the safety check refuses: it stands still while the wind moves everything else; its hair hangs straight when the mist streams; it is too far to have been there a moment ago; its feet are hidden by mist; it is seen only in the bronze mirror, not in the room.
