## Description
Hài "vô lý đầu" kiểu Châu Tinh Trì: kẻ thất bại tự tin thái quá, chuyện vô lý diễn cực nghiêm túc, bẻ lái khó đoán, vật lý hoạt hình, giọng thoại lầy lội.

## Structure
### hook: Tuyên bố hoành tráng
In the first seconds the loser hero states a grand, deadly serious claim about himself or his plan, while something small and ridiculous already contradicts it.

### setup: Mục tiêu đời thường
One clear, relatable goal (rent, food, a girl, an exam, a boss) that every absurd beat will interrupt; plant one odd phrase or action to call back later.

### escalation: Thảm họa leo thang
One small failure triggers a bigger one, three times, each faster and more cartoonish; the hero stays smug through all of it.

### turn: Bẻ lái khó đoán
One big tonal swerve: a sincere, sad or epic moment played straight, then punctured by a ridiculous detail; or a nobody (a street vendor, a granny) revealed as the hidden master.

### climax: Trận chiến nghiêm túc
Stage the showdown as a genuinely epic genre scene (kung fu duel, cooking battle, final exam), then let one absurd prop or line break it.

### payoff: Callback
The planted phrase or action returns at the worst or best possible moment and wins (or ruins) the day; end on a deadpan reaction.

## Hook
A grand, fake-serious declaration from a loser, instantly contradicted by something tiny and ridiculous in the same frame.

## Pacing
Fast and stop-start: rapid banter and escalating chaos, broken by frozen silent beats; hold 1-2s on the reaction after every gag before the next cut; one big tonal swerve per film, not five.

## Dialogue
Mo lei tau in the film's language: near-homophone puns, proverbs (in Vietnamese: thành ngữ) with the ending swapped, absurd compound nouns said with total confidence, a philosophical opener that swerves into something trivial mid-sentence ("Đời người như cái bánh bao..."), grand wuxia diction ("Tại hạ...", "Ngươi...") collapsing into street slang, and rapid-fire bickering that a deadpan one-liner ends. Original lines only, never quotes from the films.

## Camera
Wide-angle close-ups on outraged or smug faces with fast push-ins; snap zoom onto the eyes before a stupid line; whip pan onto a reveal; slow motion for mundane acts treated as sacred (picking up a coin); centred, symmetrical framing for "epic" moments so the puncture lands; a static close-up held on the reaction; a frozen tableau where only one small thing moves.

## Lighting
Bold, saturated genre light played straight: dramatic rim light and dust in the air for fake-epic moments, harsh noon sun or flickering neon for street life; never dim the joke.

## Sound
A solemn orchestral or guzheng and erhu theme under a trivial act, then a hard cut to silence, a one-second hold, and one small sound (a cricket, a tiny pop); cartoon whooshes, boings and spring sounds on physical gags; everything else realistic.

## Avoid
Never wink at the camera or explain the joke; no humour that mocks appearance, disability or a group of people (an odd-looking character is played sincerely, the loser hero takes the pain); no random chaos without a goal it interrupts; no recycled famous quotes; body humour stays light and non-graphic. Video models break on many simultaneous movements, crowds, fast fights and tiny faces; they refuse gore, explicit violence, intimacy and minors in danger. Suggest, do not show: shadows, sounds, reactions, the moment before and after.

## Serial
End on the loser hero smugly announcing an even grander plan, while the next disaster is already visible behind him.

## Voice
cheeky, smug Hong Kong comedy dub: fast and run-on but drawn-out and elastic on key words, rising smugly at the end, cocky interjections (Ê, Hử, Á à, Hê hê), dead serious about nonsense
