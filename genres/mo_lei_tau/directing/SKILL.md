# Module: Directing

# 9. PHYSICAL COMEDY

Physical comedy must have choreography.

Do not simply say:

> "He falls down hilariously."

Instead specify:

```text
Character confidently steps forward.

His foot lands on a wet tile.

He slips slightly but recovers.

He pretends nothing happened.

He takes another step.

The second foot slips.

He spins unexpectedly.

His arms desperately search for balance.

He grabs the curtain.

The curtain comes down.

He remains standing.

He looks around proudly.

Beat.

The curtain rod falls onto his head.
```

This creates multiple beats.

---

# 14. CAMERA IS PART OF THE JOKE

Never treat cinematography as decoration.

The camera must contribute to comedy.

Ask:

> "What camera choice makes this moment funnier?"

---

# 15. COMEDIC CAMERA LANGUAGE

Use intentionally.

## WIDE SHOT

Useful for:

* physical comedy
* showing full-body behavior
* revealing absurd spatial relationships
* letting the audience discover something

## CLOSE-UP

Useful for:

* deadpan reaction
* awkward silence
* realization
* tiny facial change
* punchline

## EXTREME CLOSE-UP

Useful for:

* ridiculous seriousness
* dramatic object
* tiny detail treated like an epic event

## SUDDEN PUSH-IN

Use when:

* someone realizes something
* an absurd detail becomes important
* a character says something unexpectedly stupid

## CRASH ZOOM

Use sparingly.

Especially effective when:

* someone reveals shocking information
* an insignificant object becomes important
* a character notices something ridiculous

## WHIP PAN

Useful for:

* sudden interruption
* reaction reveal
* chaotic movement
* unexpected character entrance

## POV

Useful for:

* revealing what the character sees
* visual misunderstanding
* joke reveal

## HOLD

Sometimes the funniest camera move is:

> Don't cut.

Hold on the character.

Let the awkwardness grow.

---

# 16. CAMERA CONTRAST

A ridiculous event can become funnier if filmed like an epic action movie.

Example:

```text
A character prepares to pick up a spoon.

Use:
- low-angle hero framing
- dramatic push-in
- intense backlight
- slow camera movement
- epic orchestral build

He picks up the spoon.

Silence.

The spoon is plastic and immediately breaks.
```

The humor comes from the mismatch between:

```text
CINEMATIC IMPORTANCE
vs.
ACTUAL IMPORTANCE
```

---

# 17. SERIOUS CINEMATOGRAPHY FOR STUPID EVENTS

Use cinematic language aggressively for trivial actions.

Examples:

* opening a refrigerator
* putting on socks
* picking up a coin
* waiting for an elevator
* trying to sit down
* drinking tea

Treat the action as if it were the climax of an action film.

This contrast creates comedy.

---

# 18. REACTION SHOTS

Reaction shots are essential.

After a major comedic event, consider cutting to:

1. person who caused it
2. victim
3. bystander
4. completely unrelated person

The fourth reaction can be the funniest.

Example:

```text
Building collapses.

Hero freezes.

Villain freezes.

Crowd screams.

CUT TO:

An old man calmly eating noodles.

He looks up.

"...Loud."
```

---

# 19. COMEDIC EDITING

Use editing as a timing instrument.

Techniques:

* reaction cut
* smash cut
* jump cut
* unexpected insert
* delayed reveal
* visual callback
* hard cut after punchline
* silence after impact
* music interruption
* cut away before expected action
* cut back after action

### Important principle

Sometimes the audience should NOT see the entire action.

Cut before the expected event.

Then reveal the consequence.

---

# 28. VISUAL REVEALS

Instead of explaining information with dialogue, reveal it visually.

Example:

Character says:

> "Don't worry. Everything is under control."

Camera slowly moves behind him.

The entire building is on fire.

No dialogue needed.

---

# 30. PACING

Comedy needs rhythm.

Avoid making every beat equally fast.

Use:

```text
FAST
FAST
FAST
STOP
SILENCE
PUNCHLINE
```

Or:

```text
SLOW
SLOW
SLOW
SLOW
SUDDEN CHAOS
```

Variation creates impact.

---

# 31. SILENCE

Never underestimate silence.

After something absurd happens:

```text
ACTION
↓
SILENCE
↓
REACTION
↓
PUNCHLINE
```

The silence allows the audience to process the absurdity.

# 34. SHOT DESIGN

Do not put an entire complicated scene into one AI video prompt.

Break the scene into controllable shots.

Each shot should usually have:

```text
SHOT ID

DURATION

SHOT SIZE

CAMERA POSITION

CAMERA MOVEMENT

SUBJECT

ACTION

FACIAL EXPRESSION

BODY LANGUAGE

ENVIRONMENT

LIGHTING

DIALOGUE

SOUND

TRANSITION

COMEDIC PURPOSE
```

---

# 35. AI VIDEO SHOT RULE

One shot should have:

> ONE PRIMARY ACTION + ONE SECONDARY REACTION.

Avoid:

```text
The character walks across the room, fights three people, jumps through a window, grabs a motorcycle, drives away, crashes, gets up, fights again...
```

This is too much.

Instead:

```text
SHOT 01:
Character walks confidently toward the door.

SHOT 02:
His shoe gets stuck.

SHOT 03:
He tries to maintain dignity while secretly freeing the shoe.

SHOT 04:
The door suddenly opens.

SHOT 05:
He falls forward.

SHOT 06:
He immediately pretends the fall was intentional.
```

This produces much better controllability.

# 38. ACTING DIRECTION

AI video prompts should explicitly control acting.

Use:

* deadpan
* restrained
* confused
* overconfident
* nervous
* fake confidence
* forced smile
* delayed realization
* suspicious glance
* restrained panic
* exaggerated shock
* awkward embarrassment

Also specify:

```text
eyebrow movement
eye direction
head turn
posture
hand movement
pause
gaze
reaction timing
```

---

# 39. COMEDIC ACTING RULE

Do NOT make every character cartoonishly expressive.

Use contrast.

For example:

```text
Character A:
Very restrained.

Character B:
Extremely expressive.

Character C:
Almost no reaction.
```

This makes the expressive character funnier.

---

# 40. CAMERA + ACTING SYNCHRONIZATION

Camera movement should follow the comedic beat.

Example:

```text
Character confidently says:
"I have a plan."

Camera slowly pushes in.

Pause.

He looks behind himself.

Camera continues pushing.

He whispers:

"...I forgot the plan."

CUT.
```

The camera participates in the joke.

---

# 41. COMEDIC TIMING MAP

When writing a scene, estimate timing.

Example:

```text
0.0–2.0s
Setup

2.0–4.0s
Interruption

4.0–6.0s
Reaction

6.0–9.0s
Escalation

9.0–11.0s
False resolution

11.0–13.0s
Reversal

13.0–15.0s
Payoff
```

Do not make every shot equal length.

---

# 44. CHAOS CONTROL

Chaos must remain readable.

Even during a large comedic action sequence:

* establish geography
* maintain character positions
* maintain wardrobe continuity
* maintain object continuity
* make the cause-and-effect chain understandable

The audience should know:

```text
WHO
WHERE
WANTS WHAT
WHAT WENT WRONG
WHAT CAUSED THE NEXT PROBLEM
```

# THE SHOT STILL (image to video)

Every shot starts from its storyboard still. Catch the fake-epic composition before the puncture: the centred heroic pose, dust in the light, total seriousness on the face, with the tiny absurd detail already somewhere in the frame. Never draw the punchline itself: the video plays it.
