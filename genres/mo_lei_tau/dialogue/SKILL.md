# Module: Dialogue

# 4. DEADPAN COMEDY

One of the most important principles.

The world should often treat absurd events as completely normal.

### Rule

The more ridiculous the situation becomes, the more seriously some characters should behave.

Example:

```text
A man is hanging from a collapsing building.

Instead of panicking, his friend calmly asks:

"Did you lock the door?"

The hanging man looks down.

Pause.

"...No."

The building collapses another floor.
```

Do not make every character scream.

If everybody reacts dramatically, the scene loses contrast.

---

# 5. CHARACTER CONTRAST

Comedy becomes stronger when characters respond differently to the same event.

Create opposing behavioral states.

### Example

```text
Character A:
Extremely confident.

Character B:
Extremely nervous.

Character C:
Completely indifferent.

Character D:
Thinks he understands everything but understands nothing.
```

When something absurd happens:

```text
A → continues confidently
B → panics
C → barely reacts
D → confidently explains the wrong thing
```

This creates layered comedy.

# 6. CHARACTER TYPES

Use archetypes as building blocks.

## THE OVERCONFIDENT LOSER

Believes he is extremely capable.

Reality repeatedly proves otherwise.

Important:

Do not make him aware that he is incompetent.

He must genuinely believe he is impressive.

---

## THE SERIOUS FOOL

Treats completely ridiculous things with absolute seriousness.

Example:

```text
The building is on fire.

He carefully checks his watch.

"We are approximately three minutes ahead of schedule."
```

---

## THE UNINTENTIONAL GENIUS

Makes apparently stupid decisions that accidentally solve the problem.

---

## THE TERRIFYING WEAKLING

Everyone expects him to be powerful.

He is actually terrified.

---

## THE CALM CHAOS AGENT

The least emotional character causes the most destruction.

---

## THE PERSON WHO MISUNDERSTANDS EVERYTHING

He interprets statements literally.

This is especially useful for dialogue comedy.

# 7. DIALOGUE ENGINE

Dialogue should NOT continuously explain the joke.

Avoid:

```text
"Wow, that was funny because you accidentally..."
```

Let the audience discover the joke.

## Dialogue principles

Use:

* short sentences
* interruptions
* misunderstandings
* literal interpretations
* awkward pauses
* confident wrong statements
* callbacks
* abrupt subject changes
* repetition
* escalating arguments
* dead-serious delivery

### Example

```text
A:
"Are you afraid?"

B:
"No."

A:
"Your hands are shaking."

B:
"That's because I'm angry."

A:
"At what?"

B:
"...The weather."
```

The final line should arrive after a small pause.

---

# 8. DIALOGUE RHYTHM

Comedy dialogue is musical.

Use:

```text
LINE
PAUSE
REACTION
LINE
INTERRUPTION
PAUSE
PAYOFF
```

Do not make every line arrive immediately.

Silence is part of the joke.

### Important

Before an important punchline, consider inserting:

* 0.3–1.5 seconds of silence
* eye contact
* confused reaction
* glance toward object
* an awkward shift of weight
* tiny facial movement

The camera should sometimes stay on the character **slightly longer than feels comfortable**.

---

# 60. DO NOT EXPLAIN THE JOKE

Trust the audience.

Never add explanatory dialogue unless necessary.

Bad:

> "I fell because the floor was slippery, which is funny."

Good:

He slips.

Silence.

Looks around.

Straightens his shirt.

Walks away as if nothing happened.
