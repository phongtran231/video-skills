# Module: Video prompt wording

- A beat may hold one small gesture for several seconds: say what keeps moving while it holds ("the flame trembles, the child's eyes follow it, the leaves sway overhead").
- Name the light and the hour softly (late afternoon golden light on stone walls, morning mist on cobblestones, moonlight and warm lantern glow).
- The camera line starts with its framing and says how far: "Extreme long shot from across the square, static, 24mm, deep focus: the whole stone cottage and the misty hills behind it, a tiny figure on the terrace" or "Long shot, static, 28mm: the whole cobblestone lane, the figure full-length and small at its far end".
- A far shot's still (the opening picture, drawn first; the video follows it) gives its composition in parts of the frame, since an image model draws a person large unless told where everything sits: "the whole cottage fills the lower third of the frame, the autumn hills the middle third, the sky the top third; a tiny figure, a speck on the stone steps at the lower left". For a long shot: "the cobblestone lane across the lower half, old facade and roofs above it; the figure small and full-length at its right end, a tenth of the frame's height". Never a still about a pose or hands alone.
- A far shot's rules line holds the distance for the whole shot, in these words: "The camera stays this far for the whole shot, from the first frame to the last: no cut, no zoom, never closer; <Name> stays a tiny figure in the whole house and the land around it". Left unsaid, the video model cut in to her after four seconds, or framed her at medium from the start although its opening picture was far.
- In a far shot the beats, the opening, the ending and the still describe only what can be seen from that far: where the figure is, how the body moves (sits, rises, walks to the window, steps to the gate), what the place does around it (leaves fall, smoke rises, the light turns gold). Never fingers, hands at work in detail, eyes, a smile or an expression: the video model comes close to show them.
- In a wide shot, say where the people are in the place and how small, and what of the place moves around them.
- The rules line: who is in the frame and stays in it, and nothing written on screen.
- The audio line: the place's soft ambience and one or two small sounds (birds, a match striking, cups clinking, church bells far off); never music, a song or a score.
