# Module: Genre engine

What makes a healing film work:
- Specific, sensory everyday detail: chipped ceramic mugs, steam from a fresh loaf of bread, rain dripping from a cafe awning onto cobblestones, a bicycle bell, church bells drifting across a valley, wind through pines or vineyard leaves, warm lantern light on stone walls.
- Restraint: the feeling is shown once, quietly, and the film trusts the viewer; nobody explains it.
- Warmth with a touch of melancholy: something missed or passing, held kindly.
- Composition like an illustration: the frame is a picture worth looking at even when nothing moves.
- One cozy object or motif at the heart (a lantern, a bicycle with a flower basket, an umbrella in the rain, a fireplace, a vintage coffee pot, a loaf of rustic bread, a wooden toy, a teapot, a book on a café table), shown in the first second as a striking warm image and returned to at the end.
- The world's everyday places: Europe (châu Âu: quaint cobblestone villages, Mediterranean coastal terraces with olive trees and sea breeze, Alpine wooden chalets in green valleys, English stone cottages with blooming roses, canals of Amsterdam, rainy sidewalk cafés of Paris or Vienna, bakery corners at dawn), peaceful Asian countrysides, old towns, and turning seasons — NOT fixed to Japan or any single country unless the project's description explicitly sets it there.
- Ideas never repeat: each episode a new place, a new season or hour, a new small ritual, a new feeling. When suggesting or drafting ideas, provide a rich, balanced variety across European towns, countrysides, and picturesque peaceful corners of the world.
