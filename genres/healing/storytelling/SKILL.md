# Module: Storytelling

# ONE SMALL MOMENT, ONE FEELING
An episode is one small everyday moment that carries one feeling, not a plot: a baker setting out the first golden loaf at dawn in a cobblestone European town, a grandmother hanging white linen on a Mediterranean terrace overlooking the sea, a birthday cake for a child whose mother is away, an old watchmaker adjusting a brass gear in a quiet workshop, a village waiting for the full moon, an old dog walking his owner home through autumn leaves, a girl reading by the fireplace in an Alpine cabin while it rains, a boy's last swim of the summer. Choose a feeling first (warmth, contentment, longing, gratitude, bittersweet goodbye, quiet joy), then the place and the gesture that hold it.

# WHO IS IN IT (vary it from episode to episode)
Not only people with people:
- one person alone, absorbed in something they love: a woman watering flower boxes on a stone windowsill in an old European alley, tending a cottage vegetable garden, an artisan woodcarver or clockmaker in their workshop, brewing coffee at dawn by a rainy window, knitting by a crackling fireplace, baking sourdough bread, reading on a porch or veranda while it rains;
- a person and an animal: someone walking an old dog along a quiet canal or misty country road, sitting with a cat in a sunny cobblestone courtyard, a girl brushing a pony in a green pasture, feeding ducks or swans by an arched stone bridge;
- only animals and the place: a tabby cat napping on warm terracotta roof tiles or a stone bench, swans gliding on a misty morning lake, sparrows perched under the wooden eaves of an old bakery, snow settling on a quiet town square;
- or a few people together: an elderly couple sharing tea at a sidewalk table, children watching a vintage yellow tram pass, neighbours chatting over a low stone wall.
A film with one person or an animal has no lines at all, or a single soft word (the animal's name).

# WHAT THEY DO (vary it too: the activity is the heart of many episodes)
Slow, hands-on everyday rituals, filmed step by step so the viewer can almost smell and hear them:
- baking: kneading and shaping bread, a tray of cookies coming out of the oven, a fruit tart or a birthday cake being decorated, cinnamon rolls rising by a sunny window, pancakes on a Sunday morning, mochi or steamed buns, gingerbread at Christmas;
- cooking: a pot of soup simmering on a wood stove, folding dumplings at a kitchen table, making jam from summer berries, a simple breakfast for one, grilling corn or fish over coals, packing a picnic lunch, a family meal set out on an outdoor table;
- camping and the outdoors: pitching a small tent by a lake, a campfire at dusk with a kettle on it, coffee brewed on a camp stove in the morning mist, roasting marshmallows, watching the stars from a sleeping bag, a hammock between two trees, rain on the tent roof, a canoe on a still lake at sunrise, a cabin or a camper van in the snow;
- and the other small crafts of a day: gardening and harvesting, picking fruit, fishing from a jetty, knitting, pottery, painting, mending, making tea.
The food, the fire, the tent are the cozy object at the heart: shown close in its warm light (steam, a golden crust, sparks rising), then the place around it.

# THE SHAPE
- Hook: a beautiful still image of the place, alive (light, wind, smoke from a chimney).
- Setup: who is here and the everyday thing they are doing.
- The small turn: one detail that tells the feeling (an empty chair, a candle lit for someone, a hand that reaches and stops, a first firefly).
- Linger: the image that stays, held long, the feeling resolved or left open (a smile, the moon over the field, the flame steady).
No villains, no danger, no twists, no explanations in lines. The feeling is in what is seen.

# LENGTH
Use the episode's length as set; fill it with fewer, longer shots (8-10 seconds), never more events.
