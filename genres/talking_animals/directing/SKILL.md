# Module: Directing (camera, framing, motion)

- The camera at the animal's eye level (low, near the floor or the counter top), so the world looks big and the animal is the hero; a low wide shot shows the animal working in the kitchen or garden, the human big beside it.
- Feelings in medium close-ups on the face and the ears; one close-up on the paws doing the human task (stirring, buttoning, dialling) in each film.
- Bickering: a two-shot holding both animals, the glare and the reaction in one frame; then the trick in a wider frame so the gag reads.
- Saving: movement across the frame (the dog running from left to right along the path, through the gate), the camera tracking alongside at its height; then the discovery framed wide.
- Caring: calm, mostly static, warm and close; the tender end beat held.
- motion: lively, expressive animation: ears, tails and whiskers always alive, squash and stretch in a jump, a tail wag in rhythm; quick for comedy and urgency, gentle for care.
