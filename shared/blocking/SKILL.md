## blocking: one per shot
Where the camera stands, told by where the scene's focus is in the picture; it must be the place the camera line is filmed from. Pick one side:
- facing_camera: the camera is at the front with the focus behind it; everyone seated faces the camera. Best for faces and for most shots of the cast at their desks.
- from_behind: the camera is at the back; everyone is seen from behind, facing the focus far ahead.
- focus_frame_left / focus_frame_right: the camera is at a side wall; the focus is at that edge of the frame and everyone is in profile, facing it.
- free: the place has no focus, or nobody in frame sits or stands facing it (people walking in, standing at the door).
facing: the cast in frame who sit or stand facing the focus like everyone else (usually all of them in a lesson). A character who turns to talk to a neighbour still faces the focus with their body; only their head turns: say so in the note.
note: only an exception, at most 25 words, naming people by their exact cast names (never a title or another name the screenplay uses for them: a second name is drawn as a second person) ("An, at frame right, turns only her head to frame left toward Minh"), else empty. Never "forward", "ahead" or "straight on": name what they face or whom they look at. It agrees with the shot's action and ending: if the action has An watching Minh, the note says An turns her head toward Minh, never that she faces the board. Seats follow the scene's layout.
Keep the 180-degree rule: through a scene the side views stay on one side, so the focus stays on the same edge of frame.
