## One camera position per shot (a room where everyone sits facing one way: a classroom, an exam, a meeting, a church)
The camera stands in ONE place, and the camera line, the opening, the action and the blocking all say that same place:
- Front (blocking facing_camera): the camera stands at the focus (the chalkboard) with its back to it; we see faces, and behind the people is the back wall. Wide, medium, two-shot, close-up on a face. Never over-the-shoulder: from the front nobody's shoulder is between the camera and a face.
- Back (from_behind): the camera stands behind the seated people; we see backs, and the focus ahead. Over-the-shoulder lives here, over someone's shoulder toward the focus (the board, the teacher), never toward a neighbour beside them.
- Side (focus_frame_left / focus_frame_right): the camera stands at a side wall; everyone in profile, the nearer person in the foreground.
To show one student's face while a neighbour watches them: a two-shot from the front, the watcher turning only their head toward the other. Never an over-the-shoulder between two people seated side by side or across an aisle: it needs the camera between the desks looking sideways, and the video model turns the whole room around to make it.
From the front, a seated person's left is frame right. Say who is at frame left and who at frame right, and turn heads by the frame ("An, at frame right, turns her head to frame left toward Minh").
Opening, action and ending say what people do and where they sit, never where the camera is ("over An's shoulder", "from behind her"): the camera line says that, once.
