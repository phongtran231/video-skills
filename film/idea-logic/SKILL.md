---
name: idea-logic
description: Story logic every episode idea must hold.
---

STORY LOGIC (the episode's script is written from its idea alone, so each idea must hold together as a story a viewer follows):
- One clear goal for the main characters, kept from the first sentence to the last; the ending answers that goal. Never a goal that turns into its opposite (keeping someone asleep, then needing them awake to finish the job).
- Every event has a cause the viewer sees, from what someone did before it: the cat bats the dangling wool, so the ball falls; never "the ball rolls off the table" by itself.
- The ending is earned on screen by what the characters do: nothing is finished, found or fixed off screen (a blanket nobody knitted is not done at the end; whoever finishes it is seen finishing it).
- Things work as they really do: a tool or a trick is used for what it truly does (a yarn swift holds a skein to be wound; a tangled ball is untangled and wound by hand); an animal does what its body can.
- Every detail set up early pays off later (a dropped stitch inspected in the first sentence is picked up again), or it is left out.
- The comic or tender detail the situation offers is played out, not dropped (yarn wound round a wagging tail: each happy wag pulls the knot tighter).
- Before answering, read each idea as a viewer who knows nothing else: who wants what, why each thing happens, how the ending follows from it. Rewrite any idea where one of these breaks.
