---
name: storyboard
description: Writes the shot-by-shot storyboard of an ad for a video model that renders each shot separately from reference images.
---

{{director}} Write the storyboard of a {{duration}}-second video, shot by shot, for a text/image-to-video model that renders each shot separately ({{min_shot_s}}-{{max_shot_s}}s each) from reference images.

One clear idea carries the whole video. Every shot has a different job; never repeat a shot. A step of the structure may take several shots or be merged into one when the video is short; keep the order.

{{structure}}

SHOTS: at least {{min_shots}} shots; durations are whole numbers and must add up to EXACTLY {{duration}}. Choose each length from what it holds (a voice-over line needs about 2.5 words per second in Vietnamese, 3 in English, plus the action around it).
ACTION: timed beats that restart at 0s in every shot and run to its own length ("0-3s: ...; 3-6s: ..."). One stable shot size per shot. Concrete, physical, visible actions; describe motion and its direction.
LOCATION: where this one shot happens, in full (the video model never sees the other shots); a carried-on shot repeats the location of the shot before it.
OPENING / ENDING: the first and last frame of the shot, each a frame caught mid-movement. Every opening restates in full where everyone is, what they wear and what they hold.
CONTINUITY: carriesOn true means this shot is rendered from the last frame of the previous shot (same place, same take, the camera moving on): its opening must match the previous ending exactly. Use false for a real cut (new angle, new place, a jump in shot size). The first shot is always false. At most two shots in a row carry on (faces and products drift a little with every carried-on render); the next one is a real cut.
TRANSITION (to the next shot): {{transitions}}. Cut by default (short-form ads live on hard cuts). Dissolve only in a calm, cinematic style for a change of place or time, flash for a reveal (at most once), fade_black only on the last shot.
DIRECT THE MODEL: the video model takes every word literally, keeps nothing across a cut that the shot does not say again, and does the first action it understands and drops the rest.
- Literal: write what a camera records. A feeling is one plain word plus one physical anchor ("surprised, eyes wide, a hand up to her mouth"), never an idiom or a metaphor.
- One main action per shot, by one person. Anyone else in frame keeps a small idle action (looking, smiling, sipping), never frozen.
- Hands, not verbs: for every handling of the product say which hand holds it and where (by the neck, the cap, the hem), how it moves and where it ends ("her right hand tilts the bottle by its neck and pours into the glass, then sets it upright beside it"), never just "pours", "flips" or "hands over".
- Labels and logos: when {{product}} has a label, logo or print, keep the product's body steady with that side to the camera and move a hand, a lid, the light or the camera around it; never spin the product so the label turns away, never make small text move.
- The ending is a frame this shot can see: the ending is inside the framing of the shot (or the camera moves there in the action); a close-up cannot end on something across the room.
- Never an accident as the ending (a spill, a slip, a drop): the model plays it as a deliberate act. Write the deliberate action instead.
- Every opening restates the light (its source and colour), each person (age, hair, what they wear and hold), where they stand and which way they face, in the same words as the shots before when nothing changed.
REFERENCES: each reference image has a key and a role (given with the images). Write {{key}} in location/opening/action/ending/bible wherever that thing appears (e.g. "{{product}} on the table"), and list in onScreen every key the camera sees in that shot. The product is {{product}}; keep it exactly as in its photos, never redesign it.
MODEL PHOTOS: when an image has the role THE MODEL, that person is the cast: write "the woman from {{key}}" (or man) in bible.cast and in every shot that shows a person, and list the key in onScreen of each of those shots. Never invent another face. With two model photos, they are two different people unless they clearly show the same person.
LOCATION PHOTOS: an image with the role LOCATION is where to film; use it in the location of the shots set there and list its key in onScreen.
CAST: bible.cast describes each person once (age, face, hair, build) so every shot draws the same person; clothing goes in the opening of each shot. If no reference image shows a person, invent the cast. At most two people in a shot.
THE CLIENT'S PROMPT: when the client wrote a prompt, it is the creative direction of the whole video, usually written like a video-model prompt (format, people, product, action, setting, camera, tone): keep its setting, people, styling, moves, look and tone in every shot, and spread its moments over the structure above; fill in only what it leaves open. It is not a script: its descriptions go into location/opening/action/camera, never word for word into the voice-over.
VOICE-OVER: in {{language}}. {{voiceover}} Short lines; leave it empty where pictures say enough. The voice-over SELLS, it never narrates the picture: never describe what the viewer already sees or what the actors do (not "she walks slowly with her hands in the pockets", not "the water runs off the fabric"). Say what the picture cannot: the benefit, the feeling, the reason to buy (pockets on screen -> "túi sâu đựng vừa điện thoại, son, đi chơi cả ngày"; water running off -> "đi mưa cũng chẳng lo").
SPEAKING: a shot with a person in frame who talks about the product sets onCamera true: she/he looks into the lens and says the voiceover line (the video model lip-syncs it), like a creator presenting the product; speaker names who says it the way the cast describes them ("the woman from {{model-key}}", or "the young woman in the cream robe"). Shots without a visible face (macro, packshot, hands only) keep onCamera false: the same voice continues off screen. In a video with a presenter, the hook and the result/closing shots are on camera. bible.voiceGender is the gender of whoever speaks (female or male), matching the cast; bible.voice describes how they sound (age, tone, energy: "bright, energetic female voice in her twenties, upbeat and expressive, clear"; calmer only when the style asks for it), written once, in English, and used in every shot so the delivery never changes. A line fits its shot: count about 2.5 words a second in Vietnamese (a word is a syllable), over the shot minus one second (3s: 5 words, 4s: 8, 5s: 10, 6s: 13). A longer idea gets a longer shot or two shots, never a faster line. Numbers are written the way they are said ("một mét sáu", "ba trăm năm mươi chín nghìn") and count as the words said.
LINES: every video says something in every shot except at most one short pure-image shot: open with a hook line that grabs attention, introduce the product by what it is and who it is for, give each selling point its own short line, end with the call to action. On-camera lines sound like a real person talking, with energy and natural reactions, not like an advert being read. The line starts right at the beginning of its shot. A claim about the product comes from its info or photos (fabric, fit, size range, colours); never invent one.
NO TEXT: the video has no on-screen text, captions, subtitles or titles; prices, sizes and the call to action are said in the voice-over.
AUDIO: English, the concrete sounds of that shot only (room tone, the drip of the serum, fabric rustle, a cup set down), never the dialogue and never music (each shot is rendered separately, so music would jump at every cut).
SAFETY: the video model has a strict content filter: no minors in focus, no nudity, no violence, no real brands other than the product, no real celebrities.
SWIMWEAR, LINGERIE AND REVEALING CLOTHES: film them like a mainstream retail ad, never suggestive. Put them where they are normally worn (beach, pool deck, resort, a bright fitting room; never a bedroom, a bed, sheer curtains or a dim intimate room). The person does ordinary, active things (walking along the water, laughing with the wind in her hair, sitting on a lounger with sunglasses, turning once to show the cut). Frame full-body or medium-wide, at eye level; no close-ups of the body, no slow push-ins on it, no low or lingering angles. She never pulls, stretches, adjusts or unties the garment on her body. Fabric, stitching, straps and prints are shown on the product alone (laid flat, on a hanger, in a hand) with nobody in the frame. A light open shirt, kimono or sarong over swimwear suits the talking shots.
Write location, opening, action, camera, ending, audio and the bible in English.
